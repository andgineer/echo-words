8_PROMPT = """You are a vocabulary tutor. The word below is in {source_lang}. Analyse 

9this word or short phrase: {word} 

10 

11WRITE YOUR ENTIRE ANSWER IN {target_lang}. 

12This is absolute. Every part of the answer — the translations, the labels, 

13the register marks, the usage notes, the origin, the explanations of the 

14examples — is written in {target_lang}, and in no other language. These 

15instructions are in English; that says nothing about the answer language. 

16Do not answer in English unless {target_lang} IS English. Do not answer in 

17{source_lang} unless {target_lang} IS {source_lang}. Two things, and only 

18these two, stay in {source_lang}: the headword itself and the example 

19sentences (each of which is followed by its {target_lang} translation). 

20 

21{context_note}Be compact. No preamble, no closing remarks. {source_hints} 

22The order of the sections is fixed: 

23 

241. First line: the word being analysed, in bold. 

252. Translations, most frequent in everyday speech first; each with its 

26 part of speech and a register mark (colloquial, formal, slang, vulgar 

27 and so on) where it matters. 

283. Usage: typical collocations and prepositions, what it is confused 

29 with; countability and irregular forms where they matter. 

304. Origin: if the word was borrowed, 1-3 sentences on which language it 

31 came from and how it travelled; for a native word, one line. 

325. Examples: 2-4 short everyday sentences, each followed by its translation. 

33 

34Give no phonetic transcription: pronunciation is delivered as audio. 

35 

36Analyse EXACTLY the word given and do NOT substitute another. If it looks 

37like a typo, do not silently fix it: add one short line beginning with ✏️ 

38and naming the likely intended spelling, and put that spelling in the 

39suggestion field of the card JSON. If there is no typo, suggestion is an 

40empty string. 

41If it is an idiom or a phrasal verb, explain both the literal and the 

42figurative sense and when it is used. 

43ALL EMPHASIS IS HTML. Use exactly two tags and no others: <b> around the 

44headword, <i> around the {source_lang} example sentences and any 

45{source_lang} form quoted inside the text. Emphasis punctuation of any 

46kind is forbidden — no markdown, no headings, no bullet markers, no 

47tables, no code fences. 

48The whole analysis: at most 3500 characters. 

49 

50After the analysis output the line ===CARD=== exactly, and immediately 

51after it one line of JSON, with no commentary and no HTML tags inside the 

52values: 

53{{"word": "...", "suggestion": "...", 

54 "meanings": [{{"label": "...", "pos": "...", "translations": ["...", "..."], 

55 "examples": [{{"text": "...", "translation": "..."}}]}}]}} 

56word — the input word exactly as given (never corrected); suggestion — the 

57likely intended spelling of a typo, or an empty string. 

58meanings normally holds one element with an empty label. Split it into 

59several (at most three) only when the senses are genuinely unrelated (like 

60bank "financial institution" and bank "river edge"); then label is a 

611-3 word tag in {target_lang} telling the senses apart. pos is that 

62sense's part of speech as one short abbreviation in {target_lang}. 

63translations — the 2-4 main {target_lang} translations of that sense; 

64examples — the 1-2 shortest examples of that very sense: text is a 

65sentence in {source_lang}, translation is its rendering in {target_lang}. 

66In at least one example per sense, use the headword in exactly the form it 

67was given, unless that makes the sentence unnatural. 

68 

69Before you answer, check two things once more. Is every word of it in 

70{target_lang}, except the headword and the {source_lang} example sentences? 

71And is every emphasis an HTML tag, with no punctuation used for emphasis 

72anywhere? 

73"""

83word or short phrase in depth: {word} 

84 

85WRITE YOUR ENTIRE ANSWER IN {target_lang}, and in no other language. These 

86instructions are in English; that says nothing about the answer language. 

87Only the headword and the example sentences stay in {source_lang}, and 

88every example is followed by its {target_lang} translation. 

89 

90{context_note}The reader has already seen the short entry, so do not skimp 

91on detail — but stay on the point in every section. 

92 

931. First line: the word being analysed, in bold. 

942. EVERY sense, not only the frequent ones: figurative, domain-specific, 

95 archaic, regional and slang alike. For each, the part of speech, the 

96 register mark, and the field it belongs to. If a sense lives only in a 

97 particular domain (law, medicine, sport, jargon), name that domain. 

983. Origin, in depth: the source language, the original form and meaning, 

99 the route the word travelled, when it entered the language, related 

100 words within the language and cognates elsewhere. For a native word,

1034. Usage: set phrases, government, register, what it is confused with,

1055. Shades and near-synonyms: how it differs from them and which is apt

1076. Examples: one or two per sense from section 2, each followed by its

109 

110For emphasis use ONLY the HTML tags <b> and <i>: the headword in bold, the 

111{source_lang} examples in italics. No markdown, no other tags. Give no 

112phonetic transcription. No JSON and no delimiters of any kind — this is 

113reading matter only. 

114"""

117def build_prompt(language: Language, word: str, target_lang: str, *, context: str = "") -> str: 

118 """Build the compact analysis prompt.""" 

119 context_note = _CONTEXT_NOTE.format(context=context) if context else "" 

120 return _PROMPT.format( 

121 source_lang=language.name, 

122 target_lang=target_lang, 

123 source_hints=language.prompt_hints or "", 

124 word=word, 

125 context_note=context_note, 

126 )

146def extract_card(raw: str, word: str, language: Language) -> ParsedCard | None: 

147 """Extract and validate the hidden card block, returning ``None`` when it is unusable.""" 

148 delimiter_at = raw.find(CARD_DELIMITER) 

149 if delimiter_at < 0: 

150 return None 

151 payload = raw[delimiter_at + len(CARD_DELIMITER) :] 

152 try: 

153 return parse_card_payload(payload, word, language) 

154 except CardParseError: 

155 return None
